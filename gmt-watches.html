Why a GMT Watch?

A GMT watch tracks two time zones simultaneously — your local time on the standard 12-hour dial, and a second zone displayed on a 24-hour hand pointing to a bezel ring. Originally developed for Pan Am pilots in 1954, the GMT has become the go-to complication for anyone who crosses time zones regularly or works with international teams.

The watches below span every tier from the Timex Waterbury at under $150 to the Patek Philippe Annual Calendar GMT at contact-dealer territory. Whether you want a practical daily tool or the watch that started it all, there's a GMT here for you.

What Is a GMT Watch?

GMT stands for Greenwich Mean Time — the baseline from which all time zones are measured. A GMT watch adds a 24-hour hand that completes one rotation per day, pointing to a fixed bezel ring. Set it to UTC or a home time zone, read local time on the 12-hour dial, and you instantly know the time in two places at once.

There are two types: a true GMT (caller GMT) lets you adjust the local hour hand independently without stopping the movement — essential when crossing time zones. A standard GMT moves both hands together; you adjust via the bezel. Both work, but the caller GMT is more practical for frequent travelers.

The GMT complication was invented for Pan Am pilots in 1954, who needed to track New York time while flying to London. Rolex developed the first GMT-Master for the airline — and the rest is horological history.

GMT Buying Guide

Under $500: Quartz is Smart

The Timex Waterbury and Ratio FreeDiver are the standouts — genuine dual-time function, no compromise. For automatic under $500, the Spinnaker Dumas and Seiko SSK001 are the best-value options available.

True GMT vs. Standard GMT

For frequent travelers, hold out for a caller GMT — adjusting the local hour hand without stopping the movement is a real practical advantage. The Seiko 6R64 and Tudor MT5652 both offer this at accessible prices.

Bezel Color: Form Meets Function

Pepsi (red/blue) splits day and night visually on the 24-hour ring. Batman (black/blue) is more understated. All-black is the most versatile. All work equally well mechanically — choose based on how you'll wear it.

Case Size & Wearability

Most GMTs run 40–44mm due to the extra dial information. The 40mm Rolex and 41mm Raymond Weil wear more elegantly under a shirt cuff. A 44mm dive-style GMT like the Spinnaker suits a bolder, sportier look.
