Why Buy a Microbrand?

A microbrand is a small, independent watch company — typically founder-led, direct-to-consumer, and operating without the overhead of a traditional Swiss watch group. They don't have boutiques in Geneva airport or six-figure marketing budgets. What they do have is a direct relationship with their customers, obsessive attention to the things that matter — finishing, dial quality, movement choice, strap comfort — and prices that reflect what a watch actually costs to make, not what a brand name is worth.

The best microbrands produce watches that compete with Swiss and Japanese watches at three to five times the price. Many use the same movements found in Tissot, Longines, and even some Omegas. The difference is that you're paying for the watch, not the heritage.

The 35 brands below represent the breadth of what independent watchmaking looks like in 2026 — from $200 field watches to $1,500 dive instruments. Every brand listed ships directly to customers worldwide.

Pilot & Aviation

Big dials, legible numerals, aviation heritage
Laco
Germany · Founded 1925
$500 – $1,200

One of the original five manufacturers contracted by the German Luftwaffe in World War II. Laco's pilot watch heritage is genuine — these designs trace directly to instruments used in German aircraft. Swiss movements in German-made cases. One of the most authentic pilot watch origins in production.

PilotGermanWWII Heritage
laco.de
Gavox
Switzerland · Founded 2012
$400 – $900

Aviation-focused Swiss brand with a serious approach to legibility and function. The Squadron collection puts Swiss movements inside large, purposeful pilot cases. Less well known than it deserves to be — a genuine value in Swiss pilot watches.

PilotSwiss MadeAviation
gavox.com
Magrette
New Zealand · Founded 2011
$350 – $700

New Zealand brand with a cult following built around clean, uncluttered dials and superb value. The Moana Pacific Pilot is one of the most referenced entry points into serious microbrand collecting — excellent finishing, Miyota movement, honest pricing.

PilotDiveMiyota
magrette.com

£900 – £1,600

Britain's oldest surviving watch brand, revived in 2013. Fears produces dress watches with Swiss movements and a specifically English aesthetic — understated, clean, made for wearability rather than display. The Mappin series is quietly one of the best-dressed watches at the price.

Founded by legendary dial maker Hajime Asaoka, Kurono Tokyo produces Japanese lacquer dials and craftsmanship rarely seen outside haute horlogerie. Extremely limited production, lottery-based purchasing. The waiting list is a year long. The watches are worth it.

France · Founded 2018
€800 – €2,000

The breakout microbrand star of 2024–25. Awake's Sơn Mài collection brings Vietnamese lacquer painting — a Métiers d'Art discipline found only on six-figure watches at Vacheron and Blancpain — to a $2,000 price point. Silver leaf gilding under hand-applied lacquer creates a dial that is genuinely unique on every watch. Sustainability-first brand with recycled steel cases and bio-based straps. New Jersey industrial designer Jonathan Ferrer built a brand around his passion for espresso — every detail of the Metric Chronograph traces back to the ritual of a perfectly timed espresso shot, down to the 25–35 second highlighted scale on the dial. The Maritimer dive watch hits 200m WR with a ceramic bezel and sapphire crystal well under $300. The Commodore GMT adds a Ronda quartz GMT movement and jubilee bracelet.
